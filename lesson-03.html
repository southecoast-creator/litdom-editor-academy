✍🏽

The Author

Part 02

The Editor–Author Relationship

Working with authors requires both editorial skill and emotional intelligence. Study each principle below.

LESSON 01

Two People, One Manuscript

The editor and author may have different roles, but they are working toward the same result: a stronger manuscript.

“The manuscript is the shared ground.”
LESSON 02

Different Roles. Shared Goal.

  • The author protects the creative vision.
  • The editor protects the quality of the manuscript.
  • Both work toward a better reading experience.
LESSON 03

What the Author Brings

An author brings something an editor cannot manufacture: the author's creative vision.

  • Story and ideas
  • Character intentions
  • Personal voice and style
  • Creative purpose
LESSON 04

What the Editor Brings

The editor provides an outside perspective and professional judgment.

  • Objectivity
  • Technical knowledge
  • Reader awareness
  • Editorial experience
LESSON 05

Trust, Communication & Respect

Good editing requires communication. An editor should be able to explain recommendations clearly without belittling the author.

“Professional feedback improves the manuscript without attacking the person.”
LESSON 06

When You Disagree

  • Explain the editorial concern.
  • Listen to the author's reasoning.
  • Consider alternatives.
  • Return the discussion to the manuscript.
LESSON 07

Professional Boundaries

  • Respect deadlines and agreed responsibilities.
  • Keep manuscript information confidential.
  • Do not insult or humiliate the author.
  • Do not impose your personal writing style.
LESSON 08

The Golden Rule

“Edit the manuscript, not the author.”

Your responsibility is to improve the work while respecting the person who created it.

Part 03

When Editor and Author Disagree

Disagreement is a normal part of editing. What matters is how the disagreement is handled.

Editor:
“I think this scene could be shortened because the middle section slows the pacing.”
Author:
“I understand, but that section is important to the character's emotional development.”

What should the editor do?

Option A

Tell the author that the editor knows best and remove the section.

Option B — Correct Approach

Discuss the concern, listen to the author's reasoning, and explore whether the scene can be strengthened without losing its purpose.

Option C

Stop communicating with the author and simply make whatever changes seem necessary.

Part 04

Professional or Unprofessional?

A good editor knows that technical skill alone is not enough. Professional conduct matters too.

✓ Professional

Explain why you are recommending a change.

✕ Unprofessional

Tell the author their writing is terrible.

✓ Professional

Respect the author's creative voice while identifying genuine problems in the manuscript.

✕ Unprofessional

Rewrite every sentence simply because you prefer your own writing style.

✓ Professional

Keep communication focused on the manuscript and agreed editorial goals.

✕ Unprofessional

Attack or humiliate the author when they disagree with your recommendation.

Remember

A professional editor does not need to agree with everything an author writes. They need to communicate their concerns respectfully and provide sound editorial reasoning.
