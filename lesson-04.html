Editorial Ethics

Editing is a position of trust. An editor works with another person's ideas, words, creative work, and often private information. Professional editing therefore requires more than technical ability — it requires integrity.

Module Progress 0 / 5
Part 01

What Is Editorial Ethics?

Editorial ethics refers to the principles that guide an editor's professional decisions and behaviour. An ethical editor protects the integrity of the work while treating the author with honesty and respect.

Editing Is a Position of Trust

Authors give editors access to work that may be unpublished, personal, commercially valuable, or deeply meaningful to them. That access creates responsibility. An editor must handle the manuscript carefully, communicate honestly, and avoid abusing the trust placed in them.

Part 02

The Principles of Ethical Editing

Ethical editing rests on several core principles. These principles should guide your decisions even when a situation is difficult.

PRINCIPLE 01

Confidentiality

Protect manuscripts, private information, and unpublished material entrusted to you.

PRINCIPLE 02

Honesty

Give truthful editorial feedback rather than telling an author what they simply want to hear.

PRINCIPLE 03

Respect

Treat the author and their creative work with professionalism, even when you disagree.

PRINCIPLE 04

Objectivity

Base editorial recommendations on the manuscript and agreed goals rather than personal preferences.

PRINCIPLE 05

Ownership

Remember that the manuscript belongs to the author. Your role is to edit, not claim creative ownership.

PRINCIPLE 06

Professional Boundaries

Keep the working relationship professional and respect agreed responsibilities and limits.

Part 03

Confidentiality & Trust

An editor may see material that has not been published and may never be intended for public viewing. Protecting that information is one of the most basic responsibilities of professional editing.

Infographic 03

The Confidentiality Rule

Never treat an author's manuscript, personal information, or unpublished ideas as material that you are free to share.

Think Before You Share

Do not send manuscripts to friends for opinions, publish excerpts without permission, use an author's ideas for your own work, or discuss private manuscript information casually with people who are not involved in the project.

Part 04

Honesty, Objectivity & Conflicts

An ethical editor must be able to separate professional judgment from personal preference. You are not editing the manuscript to make it sound like you.

Infographic 04

Objective Editing

Strong editorial decisions are based on the needs of the manuscript, its intended audience, and the agreed editorial goals.

ASK YOURSELF

Is This Actually a Problem?

Do not recommend a change merely because you would personally write the sentence differently.

ASK YOURSELF

Am I Being Objective?

Consider whether your recommendation serves the manuscript rather than your personal taste.

Part 05

Professional Conduct

Ethics also appears in the everyday behaviour of an editor. The way you communicate, handle disagreements, manage information, and respect boundaries all contribute to your professional reputation.

Infographic 05

The Ethical Editor

Professionalism is not only about what you edit. It is also about how you conduct yourself while working with another person's creative work.

Remember

An editor's reputation is built through trust. Protect confidential information. Give honest feedback. Respect authorial ownership. Avoid conflicts of interest. Maintain professional boundaries. And when you encounter a serious ethical concern, handle it responsibly rather than ignoring it.
